2 · Core concept — the equation

The photosynthesis equation

Photosynthesis is a chemical reaction — like a recipe with specific ingredients and products.

6CO₂ + 6H₂O + light energy → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂
Carbon dioxide + Water + Light → Glucose (sugar) + Oxygen

Plants use light energy to split water molecules and combine carbon dioxide into glucose — a sugar used for energy and growth. Oxygen is released as a by-product. That's the air we breathe!

Where does it happen?

1

The leaf — the solar panel

Flat and wide to catch as much sunlight as possible. The green color comes from a pigment called chlorophyll.

2

Chloroplasts — the factories

Tiny green structures inside leaf cells. This is exactly where the photosynthesis reaction takes place.

3

Chlorophyll — the light trap

Green pigment inside chloroplasts. It absorbs red and blue light, reflects green (why plants look green!), and converts light into chemical energy.

4

Stomata — the breathing pores

Tiny holes on leaf undersides. CO₂ enters, O₂ exits. Guard cells open and close them depending on conditions.

Why does it matter for all life?

Photosynthesis is the foundation of nearly every food chain on Earth. Plants convert the sun's energy into chemical energy stored in glucose. Every animal — including us — ultimately depends on that energy. It also produces all the oxygen in our atmosphere.

Real-world connections

Deforestation reduces Earth's ability to absorb CO₂, contributing to climate change. Ocean algae perform about 50% of all Earth's photosynthesis. Scientists are even working on artificial photosynthesis machines to create clean fuel from sunlight!
